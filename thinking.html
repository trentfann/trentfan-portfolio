HOW I THINK

A few things nine years taught me, that I didn't expect.

Not a framework. Just what kept turning out to be true across SaaS, ecommerce, and hospitality companies in four different countries.

01

Marketing problems usually aren't marketing problems.

Most of the "marketing problems" I get called in for turn out to be measurement problems, or two teams quietly not trusting each other's numbers. Fix that, and half the marketing problem disappears on its own — no campaign required.

02

The first dashboard I build isn't for executives. It's for the team doing the work.

Executive dashboards get glanced at once a month. If the person running the campaign doesn't trust the number they're looking at every day, nothing downstream from it matters.

03

Most companies buy more software before they fix their process.

I've watched teams add a CDP, a new attribution tool, and a fourth analytics platform to solve a disagreement that was really just three people not agreeing on what "conversion" means. New software doesn't fix that.

04

AI hasn't replaced marketers. It's made good judgment more valuable.

Everyone can produce more content and more analysis now. The scarce thing isn't output anymore — it's knowing which of the fifty options the AI just handed you is actually worth doing.

05

I'd rather be the one who says "not yet" than the one who launches on schedule.

Most companies have a launch date they committed to before anyone checked whether the tracking actually works. I've delayed campaigns to fix that first. I've never once regretted it.

AUG 2026 · 4 MIN READ

GEO isn't SEO with a new name

Everyone's slapping "GEO" onto their SEO deck right now, and most of it is the same keyword-density thinking with a new acronym. An answer engine doesn't need a click — it needs to extract a clean, quotable fact and feel confident enough to cite it without sending the user anywhere.

The practical shift I make for clients: rebuild key pages around a single, unambiguous claim per section, back it with structured data, and stop writing introductions that exist purely to pad word count. It's less writing, not more.

JUL 2026 · 3 MIN READ

The CRM is the product

Most companies treat their CRM as admin overhead. I treat it as the actual source of truth. When I rebuilt the CRM architecture at FCS Solutions, the point wasn't cleaner dashboards — marketing, sales, and ops were working from three different definitions of "qualified lead." SQL growth was a downstream effect of fixing that, not the goal itself.

JUN 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Stop hiring growth marketers. Hire systems thinkers.

A lot of 10–50 person companies hire their first growth marketer the way they'd hire their fifth. At that size you don't have a campaign problem. You have nothing to measure the campaign against. The tell in an interview: ask what they'd do in week one. If the answer is a campaign idea, keep looking.
